Course overview

This course introduces fundamental data structures, including lists, trees, graphs, and maps, and the algorithms that operate on them, including graph traversal, shortest paths, minimum spanning trees, mergesort, and quicksort. The objectives of this course are as follows:

  • Learn commonly used data structures systematically, with emphasis on their abstract properties.
  • Understand typical algorithms for each structure and learn to evaluate their performance.
  • Compare algorithms for the same problem and select the most appropriate one.

Final Grade Calculation

A minimum of 50% in the weighted midterm and final exam grades is required to pass: (M + F) / 70 ≥ 50%. If the threshold is met, G = A + P + M + F + B; otherwise, G = (M + F) × 100 / 70.

Midterm and final exam

  • The midterm and final exam must be passed in the aggregate: (M + F) / 70 must be at least 50%.
  • If the midterm is missed for a justifiable reason, such as illness supported by a doctor’s note, its weight shifts to the final exam.
  • In that case, the final exam counts for 70% of the course grade and a score of at least 50% on the final is required to pass.

Written assignments and programming assignments

  • Specifications for assignments will be posted on Brightspace.
  • All assignments must be completed individually and submitted to the appropriate folder on Brightspace. Submissions by email are not accepted.
  • Submissions that are between one minute and 24 hours late are accepted with a 30% penalty. Submissions that are more than 24 hours late will not be accepted.

Labs

  • Lab specifications will be posted on Brightspace at 4:00 PM each Thursday.
  • Each lab will be graded by a TA during the lab session. Students must show their code to the TA, who may ask a few questions about the code before assigning the lab mark.
  • Students have one week to complete each lab. For example, during Lab 2, TAs will grade Lab 1A but will not grade Lab 1.
  • The lab participation bonus will be proportional to the total lab marks earned, up to a maximum of 2% of the final course grade.

Course policies

Communication

  • Students are encouraged to attend the weekly office hours held by the professor and TAs. TA information and office hours are posted on Brightspace under General Information.
  • When sending an email, please begin the subject line with CSI2110, followed by a few words describing the topic, to help ensure that your email is properly identified and read. We will respond as soon as possible, but please allow up to two business days for a response.

Attendance

Attendance is not mandatory, but students are strongly encouraged to attend lectures. Slides are primarily teaching aids rather than complete lecture notes and may not contain every detail discussed in class.

Academic integrity and use of AI tools

Plagiarism (copying and submitting someone else's work for credit) and other forms of academic fraud are serious academic offenses that will not be tolerated. Students who provide solutions for others to copy also commit an offense because they are active participants in plagiarism. Both the person copying and the person providing the material will be reprimanded in a similar manner, according to regulations established by the University of Ottawa.

For this course, using ChatGPT or similar AI tools in any submission is considered academic fraud.
